# Retail Sales Performance Analysis Using Microsoft Excel

## Project Overview

This project focuses on analyzing retail sales data using Microsoft Excel to uncover business insights, evaluate sales performance, and support data-driven decision-making.

The analysis transforms raw transaction records into meaningful information through Excel formulas, PivotTables, KPI cards, and interactive charts. The final dashboard provides an overview of revenue, profit, profit margin, order volume, monthly trends, regional performance, and product-category sales.

This project demonstrates practical skills in data analysis, spreadsheet calculations, data visualization, and business reporting.

## Business Objectives

The main objectives of this project were to:

- Calculate key financial metrics, including revenue, total cost, profit, and profit margin.
- Identify the months with the highest revenue and profit.
- Compare sales performance across different regions.
- Determine which product categories contribute most to revenue and profit.
- Analyze the quantity of products sold across categories and regions.
- Present the findings through a clear and professional Excel dashboard.

## Dataset Description

The project uses a fictional retail dataset containing 500 transaction records from 2025.

The dataset includes:

- Order ID
- Order Date
- Region
- Sales Representative
- Product
- Category
- Quantity
- Unit Selling Price (KES)
- Unit Cost (KES)

Calculated columns were added to derive revenue, total cost, profit, and profit margin.

**Note:** The dataset is synthetic and intended for educational and portfolio purposes. It does not represent actual business transactions.

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

The dashboard includes four primary performance indicators:

**1. Total Revenue**

Measures the total value of products sold.

Formula: `Quantity × Unit Selling Price`

**2. Total Profit**

Measures the difference between revenue and total product costs.

Formula: `Revenue − Total Cost`

**3. Profit Margin**

Measures the percentage of revenue retained as profit.

Formula: `(Total Profit ÷ Total Revenue) × 100`

**4. Total Orders**

Counts the recorded sales transactions.

These KPIs provide a high-level summary of the business's financial performance.

## Key Findings

The analysis produced the following findings:

1. **August recorded the highest monthly revenue.** This identifies August as the strongest month in terms of total sales value in the dataset.

2. **February recorded the highest monthly profit.** This shows that the month generating the highest profit was different from the month generating the highest revenue.

3. **Electronics generated the highest revenue and profit.** Electronics was the strongest product category in terms of financial contribution.

4. **Nairobi was the leading sales region, followed by Rift Valley.** These regions recorded the highest product sales performance in the regional analysis.

5. **Accessories recorded the highest unit sales across regions.** Despite Electronics generating more revenue and profit, Accessories sold in greater quantities. This highlights the difference between sales volume and financial contribution.

## Business Insights

The findings illustrate why businesses should evaluate sales using more than one metric.

- **Revenue and profit should be analyzed separately.** A high-revenue month does not necessarily produce the highest profit.
- **Product volume and financial contribution are different.** Accessories may sell in larger quantities, while Electronics contributes more revenue and profit.
- **Regional performance can guide business decisions.** Strong-performing regions can be studied to identify practices that may be useful elsewhere.
- **Product-category analysis supports planning.** Businesses can use category performance to inform inventory management, pricing, promotions, and resource allocation.

These insights are based on the synthetic dataset and should not be generalized to real retailers without further evidence.

## Limitations

- The dataset is fictional and does not represent a real company's sales.
- The findings reflect only the transactions included in the dataset.
- The analysis identifies patterns but does not establish the causes behind those patterns.
- Real-world recommendations would require additional context, such as marketing expenditure, customer behavior, stock availability, and operating costs.

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates how Microsoft Excel can be used to transform raw sales records into a structured analysis and an informative dashboard.

By combining formulas, PivotTables, charts, and KPI cards, the project highlights differences in monthly performance, regional sales, product-category volume, and financial contribution.

It also demonstrates the importance of interpreting multiple metrics together when evaluating business performance.
